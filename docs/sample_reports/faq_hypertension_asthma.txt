Common Conditions FAQ — Hypertension and Asthma

What is hypertension?
Hypertension, or high blood pressure, is a condition in which the force of blood against the artery walls is consistently too high. It's often called a "silent" condition because it usually has no obvious symptoms, but over time it increases the risk of heart disease, stroke, and kidney problems. Blood pressure is measured as two numbers: systolic (pressure when the heart beats) over diastolic (pressure when the heart rests between beats).

What are common symptoms of hypertension?
Most people with high blood pressure have no symptoms at all, which is why regular checkups matter. In severe cases, symptoms can include headaches, shortness of breath, or nosebleeds, but these usually only appear once blood pressure is dangerously high.

How is hypertension managed?
Management typically combines lifestyle changes — reducing salt intake, regular exercise, maintaining a healthy weight, limiting alcohol, and managing stress — with medication when needed, such as ACE inhibitors, calcium channel blockers, or diuretics. Regular monitoring is important since treatment often needs to be adjusted over time.

What is asthma?
Asthma is a chronic condition in which the airways become inflamed and narrowed, making it harder to breathe. It's triggered by different factors in different people, including allergens, cold air, exercise, respiratory infections, or stress.

What are common symptoms of asthma?
Common symptoms include wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and coughing, especially at night or early morning. Symptoms can range from mild and occasional to severe and frequent, and can flare up suddenly during an "asthma attack."

How is asthma managed?
Asthma is typically managed with two types of inhalers: a quick-relief (rescue) inhaler for sudden symptoms, and a long-term controller inhaler to reduce inflammation and prevent flare-ups. Identifying and avoiding personal triggers is also an important part of long-term management. Patients with frequent or severe symptoms should have an asthma action plan developed with their doctor.

When should someone seek emergency care for asthma?
Seek emergency care immediately if a rescue inhaler isn't relieving symptoms, if the person is struggling to speak in full sentences due to breathlessness, if lips or fingertips turn bluish, or if symptoms are rapidly worsening.
